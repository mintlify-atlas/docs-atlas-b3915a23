## What is pgvector?

`pgvector` is a Postgres extension that adds a native `vector` data type and distance-function operators to your database. Instead of running a separate vector store alongside Postgres, you keep your embeddings and relational data in one place. Neon supports `pgvector` on every plan with no additional configuration required.

Key capabilities include:

- **Exact and approximate nearest-neighbor search** — sequential scans for 100% recall on small datasets; HNSW or IVFFlat indexes for fast ANN search on large ones.
- **Multiple distance metrics** — L2 (`<->`), inner product (`<#>`), cosine (`<=>`), L1 (`<+>`), Hamming, and Jaccard.
- **Multiple vector types** — `vector` (up to 2,000 dimensions), `halfvec` (up to 4,000 dimensions), `bit` (up to 64,000 dimensions), and `sparsevec`.
- **Full Postgres compatibility** — ACID compliance, JOINs, point-in-time recovery, and every other Postgres feature work alongside your vector columns.

## AI concepts

Embeddings are arrays of floating-point numbers that represent the features or semantic meaning of a piece of data. By computing the distance between two embeddings you can measure how similar they are. This is the foundation of vector similarity search and is used in information retrieval, recommendation systems, natural language processing, and anomaly detection.

You generate embeddings with an LLM API — for example, OpenAI's `text-embedding-3-small` model produces 1,536-dimensional vectors by default — and then store them in a `vector` column in Postgres. At query time you pass a query embedding and `pgvector` returns the rows whose stored embeddings are closest.
