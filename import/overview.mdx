Moving your database to Neon starts with picking the right approach. The best method depends on your database size, how much downtime you can accept, and where you're migrating from. This page lays out your options so you can choose with confidence.

## Choosing a migration method

| Method | Best for | Database size | Downtime | Skill level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Import Data Assistant](/import/overview) | Quick Postgres migrations | Under 10 GB | Minimal | Low |
| [pg\_dump / pg\_restore](/import/from-postgres) | Standard Postgres migrations | Any size | Required | Medium |
| [pgcopydb](/import/overview) | Large Postgres databases | 10 GB+ | Required | Medium |
| [Logical replication](/import/overview) | Production workloads, near-zero downtime | Any size | Near-zero | High |
| [AWS DMS](/import/overview) | Multi-source or custom transformations | Any size | Minimal | High |

<Tip>
If you can't afford downtime, use [logical replication](/import/overview). For databases under 10 GB with some downtime flexibility, the [Import Data Assistant](/import/overview) is the easiest option — just paste a connection string and go. For larger databases where downtime is acceptable, choose [pg\_dump/pg\_restore](/import/from-postgres) for simplicity or [pgcopydb](/import/overview) for speed.
</Tip>

## Other migration options

- **[pgcopydb](/import/overview)** — Parallel migration tool built on pg\_dump/pg\_restore. Best for large databases where speed matters.
- **[AWS DMS](/import/overview)** — AWS-managed service supporting Postgres, MySQL, Oracle, and SQL Server sources.
- **[Migrate from Heroku](/import/overview)**, **[Render](/import/overview)**, **[Azure](/import/overview)**, **[Digital Ocean](/import/overview)** — Provider-specific step-by-step guides.
